A patch.Init()-style panel on the 10HP deck

Start from what a 10HP DSP module usually puts on its panel, place each part in a deck cell by your six rules, then read off which strip positions carry which signals. Those set where the wiring holes go, and the holes set where the chips go. This is a mock-up to plan from, not a decision.

Fitted the other way round (JPS1 top right on the front), the two strips swap. Pots and switches work either way. Inputs and outputs would then reach the far side of the PCB from their circuits, so their wires get longer.

What each strip carries

Pads per the n8synth deck: a pot's three legs on A, B and C; a jack's tip on A, its switch on B and its sleeve on D; a button or toggle on A with its return on D. D is the deck-wide ground bus, so one more wire grounds every jack, pot and switch.

Rev B channel set: patch.Init() plus what comes cheap

The board offers more holes than one panel uses, so different panels can be wired from it. Each addition below takes a spare channel on a chip that already sits in that block's zone. It adds a few passives and a short local trace, never a new chip or a run across the board.

In the top band a pot or switch in cell JPSn uses POTn or SWn, so a hole's number tells you its cell.

Two-way CV, kept

  • Six two-way channels, as Rev A. One DG412 per middle block, so each side's three switches sit beside its holes. Their enable lines come from the expander at the top, about 30 mm away; a second 595 beside the switches would make them local.
  • One fixed CV each way in the bottom blocks, from the converters' seventh channels.
  • Switch inputs, 9 to 6, one beside each top cell. The expander keeps three spare lines.

Kept, and the headroom

  • Gate inputs stay at four. Each needs its own module pin for timing, and the Rev B pin map has none to spare.
  • Headroom with no new chips: 1 more CV in and 1 more CV out (the converters' eighth channels), 2 more pots, 4 more gate outs and 3 more switches.
  • Cross-zone runs: only the bottom band's CV and gate channels reach up to the middle zone's converters, about 20 mm each.

Module, pins and power

USB end down

  • The P4's antenna points up. It reaches 17 mm past the Pico footprint, over the low top zone and towards the top edge, away from the audio at the bottom.
  • Pins meet their circuits. Power, I2S and MIDI pins sit at the bottom, by the power header, codec and MIDI circuits. Both SPI buses' pins sit at the top, by the converters and the pots ADC. The gate-input pins face the panel-left side, where the gate inputs are. Only I2C (pins 4, 5) has to run up to the expander.
  • The USB plug runs down over the bottom zone, so parts there stay low and the power header goes to the side.
  • P4 parts. The Q201 bypass and the module's 5 V feed (at least 4.5 V at up to 0.7 A) sit between the power header and pin 39. The P4's own I2C (pins 16, 17) goes to two holes in the top gap rows.
  • Camera and display ribbons leave the connectors on the P4's top face and route over the board as needed, so they don't constrain the orientation.

Power header in the lower gap

  • On the PCB's left, the outputs side, next to the module's power pins and away from the audio inputs.
  • A keyed 2×5 shroud is 20 mm long, longer than the six gap rows. It stands just inside the strip and covers the hole columns from position 25 to 32 on that side. That side's middle ground hole moves to 24 and its first bottom cell's holes to 34. Wires to the strip's inner holes at 25, 31 and 32 there run on the deck face.
  • The module sits 1 mm right of centre to clear it.

Both SPI buses stay as on Rev A. The spec (§6.1) gives the converter bus to the CV ADC and DAC alone, for audio-rate CV at about 41 kHz per channel. The pots ADC runs at 1 MHz at most, so a pot read on that bus would block the converters for a whole CV sample period. Pins 16 and 17 go to the P4, and pin 20 (free) takes CS_595, but the pots ADC's data line still needs a pin. EXP_INT is dropped (agreed 2026-10-25): the firmware polls the expander, and gate input 3 takes its pin. On the P4, pins 21, 22 and 24 carry only signals the module drives, so a programmable bank left off or at 1.8 V can no longer have 3.3 V forced into it. The converters still need the one-line firmware fix before they work. Rev B is the Pico 2 / ESP32-P4 board; the Teensy board stays at Rev A.

What it means for the board

  • A hole level with every signal pad. Signals land on each cell's A pad (positions 1, 4, 7, 17, 20, 23, 32, 35, 38) and on B for a pot's wiper. A channel hole beside each one makes every panel wire a short sideways hop.
  • The same set on both sides. Each strip has 9 cells and 11 signal wires. Pots and switches in the top band on both sides, so the deck works fitted either way round.
  • Zones follow the bands. Pots ADC, pot reference and switch expander at the top. CV channels in the middle, two per side; they are switchable, so each side holds whole channels. Gate inputs on the PCB right, gate outputs and the 595 on the PCB left. Codec at the bottom centre, inputs right, outputs left.
  • The power header has to move. On Rev A it sits at positions 33–37 next to the PCB's right strip, exactly where the audio-in holes need to go. Rev B puts it in the lower gap instead.
  • Utility holes in the gap rows (10–16 and 26–31), where the deck has no pads: screen I2C and LEDs in the top gap, MIDI and gate expansion in the lower one.
  • More holes than one panel uses. The channel set above keeps about Rev A's count (34 against 35) but spends it on cheap channels, so other panels can be wired from the same board.

Variants and open questions

  • MIDI. Swap JPS8 (CV in 2, panel left) for MIDI in and JPS11 (CV out 2, panel right) for MIDI out. Ins stay left and outs right, and the MIDI circuit holes sit in the lower gap rows. A TRS jack uses A and B (tip and ring).
  • Screen. It mounts in the front panel; a panel with one leaves the cells it covers empty. Its I2C holes are in the top gap rows.
  • Module. In the middle, USB end down: see "Module, pins and power".
