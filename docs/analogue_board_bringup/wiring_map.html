Wiring holes, edge strips and deck cells

Seen from the outward (module) face, as KiCad draws it: from the deck side, left and right swap. Data read from the routed boards and the 10HP deck file on 2026-10-23.

Fitted as agreed, 2026-10-31: the deck built the n8synth way (JPS1 top left on the front panel) and the board upright behind it. Of the four ways the two boards can meet, this one needs the least hand wire for the first build.

Outer and inner strip holes: joined

On the routed Teensy board, all 67 positions that have an inner hole have it joined to the outer hole (the deck pin) by a 2.5 mm track: 40 on the left, 27 on the right. Nothing else on the board touches the strips, so every panel signal is a wire you add from an inner hole to a channel hole.

Right strip gaps

The right strip has no holes at 11–15 and 26–30; some channel holes sit there instead (columns RSI and RS). At 10, 16 and 31 it keeps only the outer hole, which connects to nothing.

Ground

Ground holes are square. Every deck cell's D pad sits on the deck-wide ground bus, so one wire from a D pad to any square hole grounds every jack and pot on the deck.

Deck cells

The 10HP deck's 18 cells as the front panel reads. Each cell's A, B and C pads land on three strip positions; the middle column alternates between the strips row by row. The board map is seen from the module side, so a cell on the panel's left reaches the PCB's right-hand strip. Under each cell are the holes level with its pads or, with a panel chosen, its part, its wires and what its other legs do on the deck.

On the Teensy board, cells 13 to 18 (positions 32–40) have no channel holes beside them on either side: the left has MIDI, LED and gate expansion holes there, and the right column stops at 30, above the power header.

Panel wiring

Every wire runs from a strip's inner hole, level with its pad, to the nearest free hole it may take, by straight hand-wire length. Channel names are firmware labels, so which CV is pitch 2 or which audio input is L does not matter. Gates and pitches are CV outputs (the two-way channels set as outputs; Rev B's fixed CVO7 counts too), and drums, rst and clk are the four gate outputs. A jack's tip is on its A pad and its sleeve goes to the cell's D pad on the deck.

The encoder (Bourns PEC11R): its A, C (common) and B pins sit on the cell's A, B and C pads. A and B go to two gate inputs, not through the I2C expander, whose bus the screen holds for about 23 ms per update. On Rev B only gate inputs 1 and 2 (GP6, GP7) are neighbouring pins, as Pico PIO decoding needs. The common goes to 3.3 V, never to D, which is ground. This panel has no pots, so it takes the pot reference (POT_REF, labelled REF in the bring-up pictures), much nearer than +3V3D; if pots are added later, move it to +3V3D. The push switch's two pins sit on the far side of the body: one is linked on the deck to a pad borrowed from the cell beside it, the other goes to D. MIDI in: tip (A) to MI_T, ring (B) to MI_R. Leave the sleeve open or wire it to MI_S, never to D (no ground loop).

Borrowed pads, the screen and ground (2026-10-31). The screen sits in the panel over the first two cells, so their six pads are free. The first carries the screen's SDA, SCL and 3V3 to the display holes (J703); its GND goes to D. The second carries the encoder's push and the deck's one ground link: a pad bridged to D on the deck, wired to a bank's ground hole. Every D pad is on one deck-wide bus, so that one wire grounds the whole deck. No wire runs between the boards outside the strips.

The banks on this board

No standard bank

On the Teensy board (Rev A) the four banks were packed to suit routing, not laid out to one pattern:

  • Different mix. CVs run 3, 1, 1, 1 (CV1, CV2 and CV5 are all top left). Pots run 2, 2, 2, 0. Switches run 3, 2, 2, 2. Audio inputs are in the top banks and outputs in the lower ones.
  • Different shape. The left banks fill two columns row by row; the right banks are one column, partly moved into the strip's empty positions.
  • Split banks. On the Teensy board the right-middle bank's CV6, AOUT2 and GIN4 sit at RS 13–15, ten rows above its GOUT4, SW8, SW9 and ground at R 24–27.
  • Ground and reference move about. The ground hole is LI 5, LI 24, RS 12 or 15, and R 27. POT_REF is LO 5, LO 24, and R 7 or RS 11.
  • A block has no silkscreen label. W8: there was no room.
The Rev B proposal: Rev B took it up with one column a side

A standard bank

  1. One template, same slots everywhere. Take the left-top order: CV, CV, audio, gate in, pot, pot, pot reference, ground, gate out, switch, switch, switch. A bank that lacks a channel leaves that slot empty instead of closing up, so ground is always in the same place.
  2. Each bank level with a band of three cells (positions 1–9, 17–25 or 32–40), so every cell has its channels beside it.
  3. Mirror the right side, with the column nearest the strip on the outside. Two columns on the right cost 2.54 mm of the lane U501, U502 and U801 sit in, so this trades against routing space. A single right column in the same order is the cheaper option.
  4. The same field on both boards. Fix the field first and place the chips around it, instead of moving holes per board.
  5. Number channels bank by bank, so each bank holds consecutive numbers (CV1–2 top left, CV3–4 next, and so on).
  6. Utility holes in the gap rows 10–16 and 26–31, where the deck has no pads: MIDI, LEDs, gate expansion, spare GPIO.
